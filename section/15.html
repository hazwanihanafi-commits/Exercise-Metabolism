02 • VISUAL STORY: WHAT CHANGES?

How to read the figure: the curves are conceptual, not athlete-specific measurements. They are designed to show that variables rise and fall on different time courses.

03 • STAGE 1 — REST

The baseline

HR: 60 bpm in the source example.

VO₂: resting.

ATP demand: low.

Main emphasis: oxidative metabolism.

Key reasoning: ATP demand is continuous but relatively low. Oxidative metabolism supports routine cellular work and a mixture of substrates contributes.

04 • STAGE 2 — EXERCISE STARTS

The first problem is speed

At exercise onset, ATP demand rises immediately.

Stored ATP and PCr respond rapidly, while glycolysis and oxidative metabolism increase too.

Question: Why cannot oxidative metabolism simply jump instantly to the full new demand?
Model answer: The exercise demand changes immediately, whereas oxygen consumption and oxidative ATP production rise progressively toward the new requirement. Rapid pathways therefore help bridge the immediate gap.

The values above are the example stage values in the source material; they are not universal normal values for all athletes.

06 • STAGE 4 — 30–60 MIN

Sustained exercise changes the story

With continued exercise, oxygen consumption has had time to respond to the ongoing demand.

In the source example, HR is ≈145 bpm, ATP demand is moderate-high, VO₂ is high, and the main emphasis is oxidative metabolism.

Reasoning: duration matters. The athlete now needs ATP supply that can be sustained for much longer than the initial exercise-onset phase.

07 • STAGE 5 — 2 H RECOVERY

What is still happening?

HR: 75 bpm in the source example and falling.

VO₂: falling.

ATP demand: low compared with exercise.

Main emphasis: recovery oxidation.

Recovery processes continue: PCr resynthesis, lactate handling, temperature regulation and restoration of energy stores.

Think: Why is “HR = 145 bpm” incomplete without knowing what the athlete is doing?
Model answer: A physiological value only becomes meaningful in context. HR of 145 bpm could reflect different demands depending on the activity, duration, fitness, environment and stage of exercise. Interpret the variable together with workload and time.

10 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “As soon as exercise stops, everything returns to rest.”

No. HR and VO₂ can remain elevated while recovery processes continue.

❌ “One measurement tells the whole metabolic story.”

No. Interpret variables in relation to exercise demand and time.

12 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

REMEMBER THIS JOURNEY:

Rest → ATP demand is relatively low.
Exercise starts → ATP demand rises immediately.
0–5 min → rapid ATP support + rising oxidative contribution.
30–60 min → sustained demand with strong oxidative support.
2 h recovery → HR and VO₂ fall, but restoration continues.
