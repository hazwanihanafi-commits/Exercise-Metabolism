READ THE PHYSIOLOGY

Numbers are not the answer. Your task is to identify the pattern, connect it to exercise demand, and explain what the pattern can — and cannot — tell you.

DATA REASONING → OBSERVE → DESCRIBE → EXPLAIN → INTERPRET IN CONTEXT

01 • HOW TO READ A PHYSIOLOGY GRAPH

Step 1 — Observe

What actually changes in the dataset?

Step 2 — Describe

State the direction and pattern without explaining it yet.

Step 3 — Explain

Connect the pattern to exercise demand, metabolism or recovery.

Step 4 — Interpret

Ask what the data support and what they do not prove.

Think again: Which interpretation fits the source explanation?
Model explanation: Glycolysis occurs at rest too. With increasing intensity, carbohydrate breakdown and glycolytic flux rise, and lactate appearance can rise when production exceeds immediate utilisation/clearance.

07 • DATA CONNECTION: ONE VARIABLE ≠ WHOLE PHYSIOLOGY

08 • DON'T JUST MEMORISE — WRITE THE DATA ANSWER

Use this structure:

1. Describe: “As workload/intensity increased, …”

2. Identify: “The variable showed …”

3. Explain: “This is consistent with …”

4. Limit: “This does not by itself prove …”

Model: As exercise intensity increased from rest to very hard, blood lactate increased from 1.0 to 10.4 mmol/L. The increase became larger at higher intensities. This pattern is consistent with increased glycolytic flux and lactate appearance as exercise demand rises. The dataset alone does not prove that lactate is the sole cause of fatigue.

09 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “A rising lactate graph means glycolysis switched ON.”

The source explicitly says glycolysis occurs at rest too; relative glycolytic flux changes.

❌ “One graph tells us everything.”

Interpret the variable in relation to workload, exercise intensity, time and the broader physiological context.

11 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

READ THE PATTERN, NOT JUST THE NUMBER.

VO₂ generally rises as workload rises.
Lactate in the source dataset rises as exercise intensity rises.
Glycolysis does not “appear” only at high intensity; relative glycolytic flux changes.
Data must be interpreted in relation to exercise workload, time and context.
