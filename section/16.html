ACUTE RESPONSE: WHAT CHANGES DURING EXERCISE?

A single exercise bout creates coordinated responses across the cardiovascular, respiratory, metabolic, muscle, thermoregulatory and blood-chemistry systems.

CONNECT THE CHAIN → Exercise demand ↑ → ATP turnover ↑ → substrate use ↑ → O₂ / CO₂ / HR / ventilation responses ↑ → recovery begins when demand falls.

01 • SEE THE BIG PICTURE

Acute response = response to one exercise bout

Think about what changes during the exercise session, not what develops after weeks of training.

Question: If ATP demand rises, which body systems must respond to help the working muscle?
Model: The source links the rise in exercise demand to faster ATP turnover and substrate use, followed by increased O₂/CO₂, HR and ventilation responses. These coordinated changes support the increased metabolic demand.

04 • CARDIOVASCULAR RESPONSE

What is the response?

Heart rate rises.

Cardiac output rises.

The purpose in the source is to support oxygen and nutrient delivery to the working tissues.

Think of this as a delivery response: increased exercise demand requires increased transport support.

05 • RESPIRATORY RESPONSE

What changes?

Ventilation rises as oxygen demand and CO₂ production increase.

Reasoning chain: metabolic activity ↑ → O₂ demand ↑ and CO₂ production ↑ → ventilation response ↑

06 • METABOLIC + MUSCLE RESPONSE

Metabolic

ATP turnover accelerates.

Substrate mobilisation and pathway flux increase.

Muscle

Motor-unit recruitment rises with external workload.

ATP demand rises as the working muscle performs more work.

07 • THERMOREGULATION

What changes?

Exercise increases heat production.

Sweating and skin blood flow contribute to heat dissipation.

The source presents these as acute responses to the exercise bout.

08 • BLOOD CHEMISTRY

At higher intensities

Lactate and acid-base disturbances become more prominent.

This is an intensity-dependent part of the acute response.

Do not simplify this to “lactate = fatigue”.
The learning point here is that blood chemistry changes become more prominent as intensity rises.

RECOVERY BEGINS WHEN DEMAND FALLS
Think: Why do HR, ventilation and metabolic variables all change during the same exercise bout?
Model answer: They are coordinated responses to the increased exercise demand. Greater muscle work raises ATP turnover and substrate use; the body then increases transport and respiratory responses to support the metabolic requirement.

10 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “Acute response = training adaptation.”

No. This section concerns changes during a single exercise bout.

❌ “Only the cardiovascular system responds.”

No. Respiratory, metabolic, muscle, thermoregulatory and blood-chemistry responses occur too.
