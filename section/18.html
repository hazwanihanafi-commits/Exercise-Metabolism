TCE 504 • Exercise Metabolism • Section 18

TRAINING → ADAPTATION → PERFORMANCE

An acute response happens around one exercise bout. A chronic adaptation develops from repeated training exposure and can change how the body responds to a given workload and support performance.

CORE IDEA → Repeated stimulus → adaptation → the same or greater task can be performed with a changed physiological response.

01 • ACUTE RESPONSE vs CHRONIC ADAPTATION

ONE SESSION → ACUTE RESPONSE

Examples from the previous section: HR rises, ventilation rises, ATP turnover accelerates and heat production increases.

Time scale: during or immediately around the exercise bout.

REPEATED TRAINING → CHRONIC ADAPTATION

Repeated exercise stimuli can produce longer-term changes that alter responses to a given workload and support performance.

Time scale: develops over repeated training exposure.

02 • THREE TRAINING DIRECTIONS

The source gives three examples: endurance, high-intensity, and resistance/power training.

🏃‍♂️

ENDURANCE TRAINING

Supports mitochondrial and oxidative adaptations, capillarisation and improved ability to oxidise substrates aerobically.

Think: adaptations that support sustained aerobic work.
⚡

HIGH-INTENSITY TRAINING

Can improve tolerance of high glycolytic flux, neuromuscular power and repeated high-intensity performance.

Think: adaptations relevant to demanding, rapid ATP requirements and repeated high-intensity work.
🏋️

RESISTANCE / POWER

Can increase muscle mass, neural drive and capacity to generate high force and power.

Think: adaptations that support force production and high-power performance.

03 • VISUAL: WHAT CHANGES WITH TRAINING?

How to reason from training type

Endurance: ask what would help sustained oxidative work.

High-intensity: ask what would help repeated high-intensity performance and tolerance of high glycolytic flux.

Resistance/power: ask what would help force and power generation.

These are examples from the source, not an exhaustive list of all training adaptations.

05 • SAME TASK, DIFFERENT RESPONSE

A useful adaptation question is: after training, what happens when the athlete performs a familiar workload?

*Important: The phrase “same task feels different” is a conceptual teaching aid. The source supports that chronic adaptation changes the response to a given workload and can support performance; it does not specify one universal response curve for all exercises.

08 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “One exercise session creates a chronic adaptation.”

Chronic adaptation develops over repeated training exposure.

❌ “All training creates the same adaptation.”

10 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

CHRONIC ADAPTATION = REPEATED STIMULUS → LONGER-TERM CHANGE

Endurance training → mitochondrial / oxidative adaptations, capillarisation, improved aerobic substrate oxidation.
High-intensity training → improved tolerance of high glycolytic flux, neuromuscular power, repeated high-intensity performance.
Resistance / power training → muscle mass, neural drive, high-force and high-power capacity.
