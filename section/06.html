SECTION 06 • DIGESTION → CELLULAR FUEL

How does food become available to the working cell?

You already know that food provides substrates. Now follow the journey: digestion → absorption → circulation → cellular uptake.

FOOD→ DIGESTION→ ABSORPTION→ CIRCULATING SUBSTRATES→ CELLULAR UPTAKE
PART 1 • DIGESTION

First, food must be broken down.

Food is mechanically and chemically processed into smaller molecules that can become available as substrates.

01

Carbohydrate

Starch and disaccharides are broken into monosaccharides, especially glucose.

Fat

Lipids are emulsified and hydrolysed into fatty acids and monoacylglycerols.

Protein

Proteins are broken into amino acids.

STOP & THINK
Why is digestion necessary before these nutrients can support cellular metabolism?
PART 2 • ABSORPTION

Next, the substrates cross the gut wall.

After digestion, the smaller molecules are absorbed and can enter blood or lymph before reaching tissues.

02
Key idea: absorption does not equal ATP production. Absorption makes substrates available for transport to tissues.
CHECK THE LOGIC
After absorption, what is the next major step before a muscle can use the substrates?
PART 3 • CIRCULATION

Substrate availability depends on the whole-body context.

What reaches the muscle is influenced by recent food intake, liver metabolism, adipose tissue lipolysis, exercise intensity and hormonal state.

03

Recent food intake

Changes the availability of circulating substrates after a meal.

Liver + adipose tissue

Help regulate substrate availability reaching the circulation.

Exercise context

Intensity, hormonal state and exercise itself influence substrate mobilisation and use.

PREDICT
Can substrate availability be explained by the meal alone?
PART 4 • CELLULAR UPTAKE

Now the substrate reaches the working muscle cell.

At this point, the question changes from “where did the substrate come from?” to “how does the cell handle it?”

04
Glucose: glucose enters muscle through GLUT4. During exercise, muscle contraction promotes GLUT4 translocation independently of insulin. Glycogen can enter glycolysis after mobilisation.

APPLY IT — Think about the athlete.

A runner begins exercise. Don't say “the food becomes ATP.” Use the chain you just followed:

1 • AVAILABILITYSubstrates are available from recent food, body stores and circulating sources.
2 • UPTAKEThe working muscle takes up available substrates.
3 • METABOLISMSubstrates enter the appropriate metabolic pathways that support ATP resynthesis.

ONE-SENTENCE EXPLANATION
Explain the sequence: digestion → absorption → circulation → cellular uptake.
Model answer: Food is digested into smaller molecules, these are absorbed and enter blood or lymph, circulating substrates are delivered to tissues, and muscle cells take them up so they can enter metabolic pathways that support ATP resynthesis.
