TCE 504 • Exercise Metabolism • Section 13

ALL ENERGY SYSTEMS WORK TOGETHER

Do not learn the three systems as separate boxes. During exercise, ATP-PCr, glycolytic and oxidative metabolism contribute together. The key question is: which contribution is relatively greater for this demand?

GOLDEN RULE → The pathways overlap; their relative contribution changes with exercise demand.

01 • SEE THE BIG PICTURE

Start with the problem: the muscle must keep ATP supply matched to ATP demand as exercise changes.

What changes when exercise begins?

Muscle contraction increases ATP use. Because stored ATP is limited relative to ongoing demand, ATP must be regenerated continuously.

Think: Do the systems switch ON one at a time? Predict before revealing.
No. ATP-PCr, glycolysis and oxidative metabolism are active together. What changes is the relative contribution of each pathway as demand changes.

06 • DON'T JUST MEMORISE — FOLLOW THE REASONING CHAIN

Demand → contribution

1. What is the exercise intensity and duration?

2. How fast must ATP be regenerated?

3. Which pathway has the rate/capacity characteristics that fit that demand?

4. What are the other pathways doing at the same time?

Measure → interpret

Use physiological variables in context. A change in one variable does not prove that only one energy system is working.

Interpret the athlete's response against the workload, duration and exercise goal.

Think before you reveal: Why is “Which energy system is ON?” a poor question?
Model answer: Because ATP-PCr, glycolysis and oxidative metabolism contribute simultaneously. The useful question is which pathway has the relatively greater contribution for the exercise demand.

07 • CLINICAL / PRACTICE LENS

What should you ask?

External demand: What did the athlete actually do?

Internal response: How did the body respond?

Interpretation: What does the combination suggest about physiological demand?

Key point from this section: Do not infer a single dominant pathway from one measurement alone.

08 • HIGH-YIELD + COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

HIGH-YIELD FACTS
ATP-PCr = highest rate, smallest capacity.
Glycolytic = fast rate, moderate capacity.
Oxidative = lower rate, greatest capacity.

All three systems contribute together; intensity and duration change their relative contribution.
COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
❌ “Only one energy system is active at a time.”

❌ “If oxidative metabolism is important, glycolysis has stopped.”

❌ “The bars on the screen are exact percentages for every athlete.”

✅ Think in terms of overlapping pathways and changing relative contribution.

10 • TAKE-HOME MESSAGE

Exercise metabolism is an integrated system, not three separate switches.

The practical question is not “Which system is ON?” but “For this exercise demand, which pathways contribute relatively more, and why?”
