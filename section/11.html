ETC → Oxidative Phosphorylation

Now follow the high-energy electrons carried by NADH and FADH₂. The respiratory chain uses electron flow to build an H⁺ gradient, and ATP synthase uses that gradient to support ATP production.

PART 1 • THE BIG IDEA

Electron flow becomes a proton gradient.

The electron transport chain is located in the inner mitochondrial membrane. Electrons move through respiratory complexes, and this energy supports proton pumping from the matrix into the intermembrane space.

PART 2 • WHERE DO THE ELECTRONS COME FROM?

NADH and FADH₂ enter at different points.

Both are reduced electron carriers. Their electrons enter the respiratory chain through different entry points, but both ultimately contribute to the processes that support oxidative ATP production.

Electron flow converges within the respiratory chain and supports proton pumping.

PART 4 • ATP SYNTHASE

ATP synthase turns proton flow into ATP formation.

As H⁺ moves down its electrochemical gradient through ATP synthase, the enzyme uses that energy to support phosphorylation of ADP to ATP.

04

Input

ADP + inorganic phosphate (Pi).

Driving force

H⁺ electrochemical gradient.

Output

ATP for cellular work.

PART 5 • OXYGEN

Why is oxygen essential?

At the end of the respiratory chain, oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor. Electrons and protons are combined with oxygen to form water.

05
Key consequence: Without an available final electron acceptor, electron transport through the respiratory chain cannot continue normally. Oxygen therefore supports the ongoing operation of oxidative phosphorylation.

A runner asks: “Why does oxygen matter for my ability to keep producing ATP during sustained exercise?”
Model answer: Oxygen is the final electron acceptor at the end of the respiratory chain.
