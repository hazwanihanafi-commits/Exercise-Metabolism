SECTION 09 • METABOLIC BRANCH POINT

Pyruvate → Lactate or Acetyl-CoA

After glycolysis, pyruvate becomes a decision point. Depending on metabolic conditions, it can be reduced to lactate to regenerate NAD⁺, or converted to acetyl-CoA and enter mitochondrial oxidative metabolism.

PART 1 • THE BRANCH POINT

Where does pyruvate go next?

Do not think of pyruvate as having only one destination. The next step depends on the metabolic conditions in the working cell.

QUESTION 1Why can lactate formation increase?
QUESTION 2What happens to NAD⁺?
QUESTION 3What can happen to lactate after it enters blood?
01
Why can lactate formation increase?
Answer: ATP demand is very high and glycolytic flux increases, producing pyruvate rapidly. When lactate production exceeds immediate utilisation/clearance, more pyruvate is converted to lactate.
02
What happens to NAD⁺?
Answer: NAD⁺ is regenerated. Pyruvate is reduced to lactate using NADH, regenerating NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue producing ATP.
03
What can happen to lactate after it enters blood?
Answer: Lactate can be transported to other tissues and oxidised as a fuel, particularly by the heart and oxidative muscle fibres. It can also be taken up by the liver and contribute to glucose production through gluconeogenesis.
THE REASONING CHAIN High ATP demand → ↑ glycolysis → ↑ pyruvate → lactate formation → NAD⁺ regenerated → glycolysis continues → lactate transported / used by other tissues

KEY TAKE-HOME MESSAGES
01 • Pyruvate is a branch point

After glycolysis, pyruvate can move toward lactate formation or acetyl-CoA depending on metabolic conditions.

02 • NAD⁺ matters

Lactate formation regenerates NAD⁺ and supports continued glycolysis.

03 • Mitochondrial route

Pyruvate → acetyl-CoA in the mitochondrial matrix → TCA cycle.

04 • Lactate can travel

Lactate can be transported and oxidised or contribute to glucose production.
