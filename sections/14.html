04 • FROM REST TO EXERCISE: WHAT CHANGES FIRST?

At rest

ATP demand is continuous but relatively low. Oxidative metabolism supports routine cellular work and a mixture of substrates contributes.

HR≈ 60 bpm*
ATP demandLow
*Teaching values from the source: the example stage lists HR ≈ 60 bpm at rest. Individual values vary.

05 • 0–5 MIN: TRANSITION INTO EXERCISE

Read the physiology

HR: example ≈ 165 bpm

ATP demand: high

VO₂: high / rising

Energy contribution: mixed → oxidative contribution rises with time

Why? Oxygen consumption does not instantly jump to the full new steady demand. Rapid ATP pathways help meet the immediate need while oxidative contribution increases.

06 • 30–60 MIN: SUSTAINED EXERCISE

What changes with continued duration?

With sustained exercise, the relative role of oxidative metabolism becomes more prominent.

Example stage: HR ≈ 145 bpm • ATP demand moderate-high • VO₂ high • oxidative emphasis.

Substrate selection depends on intensity, training status and availability.

07 • MARATHON: DURATION CHANGES THE QUESTION

Do not oversimplify: the marathon is not “fat only”. The source explicitly states that carbohydrate and fat both contribute, with substrate contribution shifting as intensity, glycogen availability and duration change.

08 • ATHLETE REASONING LAB

Scenario: An athlete moves from a comfortable jog into a hard 400-m effort and later returns to prolonged exercise.

A • 400 m
B • 5 km
C • Marathon
Model: Start with the exercise demand. A very high-demand 400-m effort requires rapid ATP regeneration; ATP-PCr supports the rapid start, glycolytic contribution becomes substantial, and oxidative contribution is present and increases with time. For a 5-km effort, oxidative metabolism supplies a large share while glycolytic and phosphagen pathways still contribute. For a marathon, prolonged duration makes oxidative metabolism especially important, while carbohydrate and fat both contribute.

10 • DON'T JUST MEMORISE — USE THE 3-QUESTION TEST

1. INTENSITY

How hard is the exercise?

2. ATP DEMAND

How much ATP is needed, and how quickly?

3. DURATION

How long must that ATP demand be sustained?

Then decide: Which pathway's rate and which pathway's capacity characteristics fit the demand? Remember that the systems still overlap.

11 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “400 m = ATP-PCr only.”

The source describes ATP-PCr as supporting the rapid start, glycolytic contribution as substantial, and oxidative contribution as present and increasing with time.

❌ “Marathon = fat only.”

The source states that carbohydrate and fat both contribute, and the relative mix shifts with intensity, glycogen availability and duration.

13 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

REMEMBER:

• ATP demand rises immediately when exercise begins.
• Early exercise requires rapid ATP support from stored ATP and PCr while glycolysis and oxidative metabolism increase too.
• With continued exercise, oxidative contribution becomes increasingly important for sustained demand.
• A 400-m effort requires substantial rapid ATP regeneration, but oxidative contribution remains present.
• A marathon is a prolonged-duration case in which oxidative metabolism has the greatest total role, while carbohydrate and fat both contribute.
