06 • PRACTICAL SCENARIO — REPEATED HIGH-POWER EFFORTS

Question

If your goal is repeated high-power efforts, would you use the same work:recovery ratio as a low-intensity endurance session?

Model answer: Not necessarily. Repeated high-power efforts create a much greater need for rapid ATP regeneration. Recovery duration should therefore be considered in relation to the physiological goal, the expected ATP-regeneration rate and the desired quality of repeated efforts. A low-intensity endurance session has a different metabolic demand and recovery requirement.

07 • PRACTICAL DECISION LOOP

1 • PRESCRIBE

Set the mode, intensity, duration, frequency and recovery to create the desired demand.

2 • MONITOR

Collect external-load data and internal responses such as HR, RPE, VO₂, lactate or power when appropriate.

3 • ADJUST

Use the response and recovery pattern to decide whether the next session needs to stay the same, change or progress.

08 • DON'T JUST MEMORISE — MAKE A PRESCRIPTION

Write a short prescription rationale:

“I chose this mode because…
The intensity creates…
The duration requires…
I would monitor…
The recovery is set to… because…”

Model: Begin with the exercise goal and desired metabolic demand. Select mode, intensity and duration that create that demand. Choose monitoring variables that show both external work and internal response. Set recovery according to the required ATP-regeneration rate and the quality of the next bout. Then use the data to adjust the prescription.

09 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “Prescription is only about intensity.”

The source also highlights duration, frequency, mode and recovery.

❌ “Monitoring means measuring HR only.”

Combine external load with HR/RPE and, when available, VO₂, lactate or power data.
