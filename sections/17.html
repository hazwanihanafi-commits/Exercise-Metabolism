IMMEDIATE RECOVERY

Exercise has stopped, but the physiology has not. Recovery is an active phase in which the body works to restore homeostasis. Different processes recover on different time scales.

CORE IDEA → RECOVERY ≠ “NOTHING HAPPENING”

02 • THE RECOVERY TIMELINE

The source divides recovery into three practical time windows.

⏱️

SECONDS–MINUTES

HR and ventilation begin falling.

PCr resynthesis progresses.

Why it matters: rapid restoration of high-energy phosphate availability.
⏳

MINUTES–HOURS

Lactate is oxidised or converted.

Temperature and cardiovascular variables continue toward baseline.

Why it matters: restores metabolic and physiological balance.
🕒

HOURS+

Glycogen restoration and tissue recovery continue depending on nutrition and training load.

Why it matters: influences readiness for subsequent exercise.

04 • HR + VO₂: DO THEY DROP INSTANTLY?

Prediction

After a hard interval, which variable should remain elevated for a while: HR, VO₂, or both?

Both. The source explains that recovery is dynamic: oxygen consumption may remain elevated above resting levels for a period (EPOC), while HR also declines progressively.
Key idea: a falling value is not the same as an immediate return to baseline.

05 • PCr RESYNTHESIS

Why restore PCr?

PCr availability supports the rapid ATP-regeneration system. During recovery, PCr resynthesis progresses.

Timeline: PCr resynthesis is one of the processes emphasised in the seconds-to-minutes phase.

06 • LACTATE + METABOLIC BALANCE

What does the source say?

During the minutes-to-hours phase, lactate is oxidised or converted.

Temperature and cardiovascular variables continue toward baseline.

Exercise physiology lens: recovery is restoring metabolic and physiological balance through multiple parallel processes.

07 • HOURS+: GLYCOGEN + TISSUE RECOVERY

Longer recovery window

Glycogen restoration and tissue recovery continue into the hours+ period.

The source specifically notes dependence on nutrition and training load.

Why it matters: these processes influence readiness for subsequent exercise.

Think: Why can recovery continue after heart rate has fallen substantially?
Model answer: Recovery is multi-process and multi-timescale. HR can fall relatively quickly while other processes, such as PCr restoration, lactate handling, glycogen restoration and tissue recovery, are still occurring.

09 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “Exercise stopped = recovery stopped.”

Recovery begins when the exercise demand falls, but restoration processes continue after the exercise bout.

❌ “All recovery variables return at the same speed.”

No. The source explicitly presents different processes across seconds–minutes, minutes–hours and hours+.

11 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

RECOVERY IS AN ACTIVE PHYSIOLOGICAL PHASE.

Seconds–minutes → HR + ventilation begin falling; PCr resynthesis progresses.
Minutes–hours → lactate is oxidised or converted; temperature + cardiovascular variables continue toward baseline.
Hours+ → glycogen restoration + tissue recovery continue, influenced by nutrition and training load.

Remember: not every recovery process returns to baseline at the same rate.
