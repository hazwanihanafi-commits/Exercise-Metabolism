WHAT SHOULD WE MEASURE?

Exercise physiology becomes useful when you connect what the athlete does with how the body responds. This section introduces external and internal load, practical monitoring variables and interactive calculations.

CORE IDEA → EXTERNAL LOAD = WHAT THE ATHLETE DOES • INTERNAL LOAD = HOW THE BODY RESPONDS

01 • EXTERNAL vs INTERNAL LOAD

EXTERNAL LOAD

The task performed by the athlete.

⚡

Power

External work demand.

🏃

📏

🏋️

INTERNAL LOAD

The body's response to the task.

❤️

🫁

Oxygen consumption.

🧠

🩸

Use both. External load tells you what the athlete did; internal measures tell you how the body responded.

08 • DATA CONNECTION — WHY COMBINE MEASURES?

09 • DON'T JUST MEMORISE — INTERPRET THE MEASUREMENT

WHAT DID THEY DO?

Start with speed, power, distance, work or another external-load measure.

HOW DID THEY RESPOND?

Use HR, RPE, VO₂, lactate, BP or another internal variable.

WHAT DOES IT MEAN?

Interpret the response in relation to workload, time and athlete context.

Scenario: Two athletes produce the same external power. Athlete A has a higher HR and RPE. What should you do?
Model answer: Do not interpret the power output alone. Compare the internal responses and consider the task, time point and athlete context before drawing a conclusion about physiological strain.

10 • COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS

❌ “One variable tells the whole story.”

Use external and internal measures together and interpret them in context.

❌ “RER is an absolute fuel percentage in every situation.”

The source frames RER as an estimate of relative substrate oxidation under appropriate conditions. Context matters.

12 • HIGH-YIELD TAKE-HOME

MONITORING = WHAT THE ATHLETE DID + HOW THE BODY RESPONDED

External load → speed • power • distance • work
Internal load → HR • RPE • VO₂ • lactate • BP • other physiological responses
RER → VCO₂ / VO₂, used to estimate relative substrate oxidation under appropriate conditions
Good interpretation → workload + time + athlete context + physiological response
