🧠 BEFORE YOU MEET THE PATIENT

A patient begins moderate exercise. What changes first?

Muscle contraction increases
ATP hydrolysis increases
ATP demand rises

Think: ATP must be regenerated continuously. The question is not whether an energy system is “on” or “off”; the question is how much each pathway contributes to the current demand.

🩺 PATIENT CASE

Patient A — Starting an Exercise Programme

Age: 58 years   |   Referral: Exercise programme for low exercise tolerance and cardiometabolic risk management

The patient reports being physically inactive for several years. During routine activities, the patient becomes tired relatively quickly. A supervised exercise session is planned.

Important: In real clinical practice, exercise clearance, contraindications, medication, symptoms, baseline assessment and the appropriate clinical protocol must be considered before exercise prescription. This learning case focuses specifically on exercise-metabolism reasoning.

02 • WHAT HAPPENS TO ATP DEMAND?

Exercise begins
→
Muscle contraction ↑
→
ATP hydrolysis ↑
→
ATP demand ↑
→
ATP resynthesis ↑

Stored ATP is limited. Therefore, the patient's muscles must continuously regenerate ATP using overlapping metabolic pathways.

Clinical reasoning point: A patient's low exercise tolerance does not mean that one energy system has stopped working. The exercise physiologist should consider the total exercise demand and the patient's cardiovascular, respiratory, muscular and metabolic responses.

03 • WHICH ENERGY SYSTEMS CONTRIBUTE?

ATP-PCrRapid ATP regeneration, especially when demand changes suddenly.
GlycolysisProvides ATP from carbohydrate through cytosolic metabolism.

Key idea: all contribute. The relative contribution depends on exercise intensity, duration, substrate availability, training status and other physiological factors.

05 • INTERNAL LOAD vs EXTERNAL LOAD

EXTERNAL LOADWhat the patient actually does: speed, workload, duration, power, distance or repetitions.
INTERNAL RESPONSEHow the patient responds: HR, RPE, BP, VO₂, ventilation, lactate or symptoms where appropriate.

08 • RECOVERY IS PART OF THE PRESCRIPTION

Exercise stops
→
ATP demand falls
→
HR / VO₂ decline
→
PCr resynthesis + other recovery processes
→
Return toward baseline

Recovery is not simply “rest”. Physiological restoration continues after the visible exercise task has ended.

⭐ CLINICAL REASONING FRAMEWORK

This is not a single “correct answer”. A strong response should show a logical connection between:

1. DEMANDWhat exercise is being performed?
2. ATPHow much ATP must be regenerated?
3. PATHWAYSWhich pathways contribute, and why?
4. RESPONSEWhat changed in HR, BP, RPE, VO₂ or symptoms?
5. MEASUREMENTWhat variables should be monitored?
6. DECISIONWhat does the response mean for exercise prescription?
